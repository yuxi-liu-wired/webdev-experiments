found haiku

poems already hiding in ordinary prose

Give it any English text. It normalizes, splits on punctuation and line breaks, counts the syllables of every word against the CMU pronouncing dictionary, and keeps the runs that fall into 5-7-5 exactly — the found haiku, written by someone who had no idea they were writing one. Tanka, tanaga and any pattern you like are in the pills below.

    How it works. Text is normalized and cut at punctuation and line breaks. Each word is looked up in CMUdict — 126,036 words, syllables counted from the pronunciation itself, not from the spelling. Words it has never seen fall back to a rules engine that agrees with the dictionary 91.5% of the time; those words are underlined in the results so you know which numbers are a guess. Numbers are read aloud before counting (1984 is five syllables). Then it looks for runs of whole words whose syllables partition exactly into the chosen shape. Poems that survive the strict rules earn badges — iambic scansion, rhyme, kigo, lipograms and stranger things — and a shininess: how rare that combination is, measured against 1.5 million real posts and discounted by word repetition. Results are ranked shiniest first. Nothing leaves your browser unless you ask it for a Bluesky account, which is fetched read-only.
